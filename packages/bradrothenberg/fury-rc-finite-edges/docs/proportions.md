# Fury proportions measured from public photographs

Neither Anduril nor the US Air Force has published dimensions of the YFQ-44A. Everything here was measured
from public photographs, which are not included here (list, credits and pages in `sources.md`). Treat every number as an
estimate from photographs, not as manufacturer data.

## Size

| Quantity | Value | How it was found |
|---|---|---|
| Fuselage length, nose tip to nozzle exit (boom excluded) | **33.5 ft** (33 to 34 ft) | Side photograph 07 carries an AIM-120 on the wing station. The missile is 12 ft (3.66 m) long and 7 in (0.178 m) in diameter. In the picture it is 0.358 of the fuselage length and its diameter is 0.0172 of the length. Length gives 33.5 ft, diameter gives 33.8 ft. |
| Check with people | 28 to 33.5 ft | People standing beside the aircraft in photograph 09, corrected for their distance from the camera. |
| Wing span | **24.85 ft** | Span / length = 0.742 from underside photograph 01 (below). |

The figures often repeated for the Fury, 20 ft long with a 17 ft span and 5,000 lb maximum take-off weight,
belong to the earlier Blue Force Technologies design. They do not fit these photographs: a 20 ft aircraft
would make the AIM-120 in photograph 07 only 7.2 ft long. An independent photo analysis published on a
drone research site also arrives at about 10 m (33 ft) length.

## Plan view (photograph 01, underside in flight, gear up)

The photograph is an oblique view. It is treated as an affine picture of the planform, fixed by four points:
nose tip, centre of the nozzle exit and the two wing tips. The fin tip, which is out of the wing plane, gives
the missing scale between span and length (two independent equations agreed within 1.3 %).

## Wing root (photographs 01, 04, 13)

The forebody chine runs straight into the wing leading edge: the wing is a mid wing at the chine height, not a low wing
under it. In the model the wing chord plane is 5.5 in above the nose-tip line at the root, with an 8 % thick root.

## Engine

Public reporting names a Williams FJ44-4M of about 4,000 lbf. The model uses the envelope of the civil
FJ44-4A as published by the maker: 68.6 in long, 25.3 in maximum diameter, 23.1 in inlet diameter, 650 lb dry.

## What is not from photographs

- The V-tail. The aircraft has a conventional tail (one fin, two all-moving horizontal tails). The V-tail
  inputs are a hypothetical variant sized to a similar tail volume.
- Everything inside: duct shape behind the lip, structure, tank layout. The duct is designed to a sensible
  area distribution (throat about 0.77 of the engine face area, growing smoothly to the engine face). The structure
  and tanks are a representative layout.
